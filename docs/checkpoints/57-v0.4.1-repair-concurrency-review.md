# v0.4.1 Repair Concurrency Review

**Date:** 2026-08-29  
**Scope:** Breakpoint 1 complete; stop before Phase 4 workspace-reindex integration.

## Findings and Fixes

1. An expired generation could renew its own lease. Progress, failure, graph
   entry, projection application, and publication now require an unexpired
   matching lease. Expired ownership is irrevocable.
2. A normal graph reconciliation could enter while repair was active. Unowned
   graph writes are now fenced while a generation is active, failed, or
   interrupted; a replacement or successful completion is required first.
3. Workspace graph status reused the structural-read gate and became unavailable
   during repair failure. Status now reads revision and analysis diagnostics
   directly, while structural evidence remains unavailable.
4. Lost-lease outcomes could claim a durable failure that the caller no longer
   owned. They now report interruption; an in-progress competitor reports active
   work rather than claiming completion elsewhere.

## Adversarial Evidence

- Two independently opened SQLite connections race for one workspace repair;
  exactly one acquires ownership and one observes it in progress.
- An expired owner cannot renew, enter graph update, record failure, or publish;
  a replacement generation acquires successfully.
- Failed generations reject unowned graph writes until replacement.
- A content-revision advance after acquisition supersedes the repair before any
  graph mutation or revision acknowledgement.
- Active and failed repairs reject explicit stale structural reads, while graph
  status remains readable.
- Same-revision graph update tokens and complete-manifest publication gates
  remain green.
